The idea

One loop does the growing

NGL spread because the link page asks for nothing. Someone shares a link, a friend opens it, sends a message, then wants their own link. Every other feature supports that loop.

Reading needs nothing

Anyone with a link lands on the content. No account, no wall.

Anonymous actions need an auto-made name

Something like quietfox4821. We assign it, you can re-roll. One tap, no email. Enough to comment and like.

Real-person actions need a real account

Posting under your name in Room, selling in Market, creating Rooms, running ads.

quietfox4821
  • Built from word lists plus digits, 3 to 20 characters (the database already allows up to 24)

Two traps in names like the ones you listed

Real people and brands. “mrbeast” is a real creator. The generator must never make names that match celebrities, brands or official-sounding accounts (admin, support, commonroom), or people will be impersonated.

Numbers with a meaning. The “88” in ghost88human3909 is a hate-group code, and 1488, 69, 420 and 666 are memes or worse. We skip those digit runs.

Filtering

Every generated name is checked against an offensive-word list after removing digits and swapped letters (0 for o, 1 for i). English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa lists need reviewers who speak them.

Two ways to post anonymously

A. No name

Shows only “Anonymous”. No handle, no rank. Best for confessions and the message-link inbox.

B. Your auto name

Shows quietfox4821 and its Anonymous rank. Builds a small reputation and makes moderation easier, like Reddit.

The auto name belongs to the anonymous identity. It is never linked to a real profile.

Decision Offer both with a switch in the composer? And which one is the default? I suggest B for feed posts and comments, and A for the message-link inbox.

Commenting as anonymous inside Room

Allowed but tucked away, behind a small option after tapping comment. A guest’s first prompt on Room is to sign in or sign up, so we collect real details.

Photo & video

Photos now. Video short and small.

Photos

  • Up to 4 per anonymous post, compressed on the device (built)
  • Strip location data from every photo. Not done yet, and critical on an anonymous app.

Video: 15 seconds

WhatsApp Status moved from 30 to 60 seconds, with reports of 90 now rolling out (secondary sources; check the app). Ours should be our own number, and I suggest 15 seconds: cheap to store, quick to review, hard to abuse. We can raise it once moderation works.

Why not 500 KB?

My own arithmetic, so test it before promising it: 500 KB over 15 s is about 270 kbps. That is only watchable at roughly 360p.

Contacts on the web

Browsers can only open the phone’s contact list on Chrome for Android. iPhones and desktop browsers can’t (sources differ on a Safari flag, so treat it as unsupported). For everyone else: upload a contacts file, type numbers in, or send your invite link. A native app can read contacts directly later.

Learning, in stages

  • Now: a weighted score from the signals above. It works with little data.
  • Next: friends-of-friends as candidates, then a ranking model trained on “followed” versus “dismissed”.
  • Later: embeddings, once there are thousands of examples.

A fancy model with no data is worse than simple weights. We switch when the accept rate says it is worth it.

Always

  • “Not interested” on every card, and we learn from it
  • Blocked and reported people are never suggested
  • All suggestions are 18+ real accounts
Privacy rules this feature must follow

Anonymous stays separate. A suggestion must never reveal that someone has an Anonymous ID, and anonymous activity never feeds the model. Otherwise this feature can unmask people.

Contacts are other people’s data. Store phone numbers only as a keyed hash, drop numbers that match nobody after 30 days, show people what they uploaded, and let them delete it in one tap. Never message a contact without the user tapping Invite. Never build a profile for someone who didn’t join. Phone numbers are easy to guess, so a hash is not real protection and the data must be handled as personal data (check Nigerian data protection rules).

Never show “you both have X’s number”. That exposes someone’s address book.

Location stays coarse: school and city only. No GPS and no “near you right now”.

Chosen “People who already have my number can find me” starts on, with a clear notice at sign-up and a one-tap switch-off in Settings. A lawyer should confirm the wording. Still open How often the pop-up appears.

Safety

Moderation from day one

Anonymous apps attract bullying, explicit content and spam. Google Play also requires in-app reporting and blocking for users and content, filtering of sexual content, and ongoing moderation. This ships before public launch.

Before posting

Photos and videos need a choice: mark as sensitive (eye icon). Text filter for slurs, threats, spam and phone numbers.

Automatic scan

An image and video classifier on every upload. Explicit is hidden until reviewed. Unsure is blurred first.

Community reports

Report on posts, comments, messages, listings and Rooms. Many reports from different people hide the content automatically.

Human review

A queue in admin, worst first, with one-click actions and an audit log.

Your three strikes

3 daysno posting or commenting. Reading still works.
7 daysno posting or commenting.
Banaccount and device.

Severe cases (child safety, credible threats, doxxing) skip straight to a ban. Every strike shows which rule, which content, and how to appeal.

“Ban their device” is weaker than it sounds

On the web there is no reliable device ID. We can ban the account and flag a mix of browser, IP and cookie signals, but people can clear data or switch browsers. A real device ban needs the native app. Requiring a verified email to post makes new accounts cost effort.

Child safety is a legal line

Hosting images on an anonymous app brings duties around illegal content and reporting. That needs a lawyer and a vetted scanning vendor. No public image uploads until this is settled.

Pro

Pay for more control, never for someone’s identity

You want paying users to learn something about who messaged them. I can build a strong version of that. The version that quietly reveals senders breaks the promise on your own public page and has already cost a competitor money.

What happened to NGL

The FTC and the Los Angeles District Attorney took action against NGL Labs. The complaint said NGL sent fake messages that looked like they came from real people, and told users a paid subscription would reveal who sent them. Subscribers got vague hints such as send time, phone type and rough location. The case settled for millions of dollars and NGL was barred from offering the app to under-18s. Details are in the sources at the bottom.

What Pro will not do

  • Reveal who sent a message without that sender agreeing
  • Send fake messages, ever
  • Imply it can unmask people
  • Hide a recurring charge or make it hard to cancel

What Pro does instead

  • Ask the sender to reveal. The sender gets a private request and can accept or ignore it.
  • Sender-chosen hints. A sender can attach “same school” or “same level”. Pro shows them all in one place.
  • Message insights: how many opened your link, where from
  • Unlimited links and prompt themes
  • Pro badge, ad-free, 2× level points and the Ultimate Pass (see Levels)

Ranks on anonymous posts?

Yes on posts that show your auto name. No on posts with no name at all. The reasons:

  • A rank is a fingerprint. A rare rank on a nameless post narrows down who wrote it. Only a few people are Sages.
  • On an auto name it is safe. The rank belongs to the pseudonym, not the person, and that pseudonym is already one linked identity.
  • Two separate ladders. Your real-account rank and your anonymous (Anonymous) rank are different bars. They are never shown together, and nothing links them. Any payment is tied to the real account in our records only.

Only promise reach we have measured

“Reach 10k people in 7 days for ₦3k” is only true once traffic proves it. At first we show an estimated range from our own last-7-day audience in that place. If an ad under-delivers, it runs longer to make up. Over-promising to paying advertisers is how you lose them.

Pricing model 2, later

The advertiser picks a budget and a goal (reach, clicks, WhatsApp chats) and we work out the rest. At scale this becomes an auction where the best mix of bid and expected clicks wins the slot, and the budget is paced across the week. It only works once there is real click data, so model 1 comes first.

Advertiser path
